# Lebanon Feminization of Poverty

An exploratory study of the **feminization of poverty and economic vulnerability in Lebanon**, using labour-market indicators, gender gaps, and geographic differences across Lebanese governorates.

> **Methodological note:** Labour-force participation, employment, and unemployment indicators are not poverty rates. In this project, they are used as indicators of economic inequality and economic vulnerability associated with the feminization of poverty.

## Project Objective

This project aims to:

- Examine economic gaps between women and men in Lebanon.
- Compare selected labour-market indicators between 2018 and 2022.
- Present female labour-force participation by governorate.
- Distinguish observed data from estimates and crisis projections.
- Provide maps and figures for research and educational use.

## Methodological Interpretation

The employment and labour-force participation gaps indicate unequal access to the labour market and may be associated with increased economic vulnerability among women.

However, a value such as **16%** must not be interpreted as the percentage of women living in poverty. It represents the employment-to-population ratio, not a poverty rate.

This project distinguishes between:

- **Labour-force participation rate:** the share of the working-age population that is employed or actively seeking work.
- **Employment-to-population ratio:** the share of the working-age population that is employed.
- **Unemployment rate:** the share of the labour force that is unemployed.
- **Poverty rate:** the share of people or households living below a defined poverty line.

## Data and Methodology

- Indicators were compiled from published UN Women reports and related data from the Lebanese Central Administration of Statistics and the International Labour Organization.
- The map uses governorate-level data for 2018.
- ArcMap was used to join the governorate boundary layer to the data table using the `ID_1` field.
- Estimates and projections are distinguished from observed survey results.
- National indicators are not presented as governorate-level indicators.
- The geographic map uses a graduated-colour classification by governorate.

---

## Limitations

- The current dataset does not include direct poverty indicators for all governorates in the same year.
- The geographic dataset is from 2018 and is therefore presented as a **2018 baseline**, not as a current estimate.
- The 2020 female unemployment value is a crisis projection, not a final observed survey estimate.
- Descriptive charts alone cannot establish causal relationships.
- Direct measurement of the feminization of poverty would require newer household-level income, expenditure, or multidimensional-poverty data.
