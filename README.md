Lebanon Feminization of Poverty
An exploratory study of the feminization of poverty and economic vulnerability in Lebanon, using labour-market, economic-opportunity, and gender-gap indicators, together with a geographic comparison across Lebanese governorates.

Methodological note: Labour-force participation, employment, and unemployment indicators are not poverty rates. In this project, they are used as indicators of economic inequality and vulnerability associated with the feminization of poverty.

Project objective
This project aims to:

•	Examine economic gaps between women and men in Lebanon.
•	Compare selected labour-market indicators between 2018 and 2022.
•	Show female labour-force participation by governorate.
•	Distinguish observed data from estimates and crisis projections.
•	Provide reviewable and reusable data, maps, and figures.

Methodological interpretation
The employment and labour-force participation gaps indicate unequal access to the labour market and may be associated with increased economic vulnerability among women. However, a value such as 16% must not be interpreted as the percentage of women living in poverty. It represents the employment-to-population ratio, not a poverty rate.

The project distinguishes between:

•	Labour-force participation rate: the share of the working-age population that is employed or actively seeking work.
•	Employment-to-population ratio: the share of the working-age population that is employed.
•	Unemployment rate: the share of the labour force that is unemployed.
•	Poverty rate: the share of people or households living below a defined poverty line.

Data and methodology
•	Indicators were compiled from published UN Women reports and related data from the Lebanese Central Administration of Statistics and the International Labour Organization.
•	The map uses governorate-level data for 2018.
•	ArcMap was used to join the governorate boundary layer to the data table using the ID_1 field.
•	Estimates and projections are clearly distinguished from observed survey results.
•	National indicators are not presented as governorate-level indicators.

Limitations
•	The current dataset does not include direct poverty indicators for all governorates in the same year.
•	The main geographic dataset is from 2018 and is therefore presented as a 2018 baseline, not as a current estimate.
•	The 2020 female unemployment value is a crisis projection, not a final observed survey estimate.
•	Descriptive charts alone cannot establish causal relationships.
•	Measuring feminization of poverty directly would require newer household-level, income, expenditure, or multidimensional-poverty data.

Reuse and attribution

Language
The analysis is based on Arabic research notes, while the project titles, indicators, map, and figure labels are provided in English to improve accessibility for international researchers and organizations.
